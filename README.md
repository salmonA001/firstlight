# Firstlight

**Watch AI nations grow their own civilization on a living 3D map.**

You don't play a nation in Firstlight. You watch the world. Hundreds of people gather food, farm, build and have children. They form tribes that grow into nations, and every nation has a council AI and a ruler of its own. Nations trade, found religions, invent technology, raise wonders, sign alliances and go to war on land and sea. You can speed up time, read the history as it happens, and step in with earthquakes, plagues or a good harvest.

![A world in the 1700s, with nations, forests, lakes and clouds](docs/world.png)

## Play

Firstlight is one HTML file with no build step.

- **Locally:** open `index.html` in a modern browser.
- **Hosted:** put the repo on any static host, such as Vercel, Netlify or GitHub Pages. `vercel.json` is already set up.

Pick a starting era from the **Stone Age** to the **2000s**. You can also enter a world seed to replay the same map.

## What happens in a world

### People
Every person has a name, an age, hunger, health and four personality traits: aggression, sociability, curiosity and diligence. Each person decides what to do next by weighing their needs against their tribe's plan: forage, hunt, farm, quarry stone, build, trade, think up new ideas, raid, or march to war. Click anyone to see what they're thinking.

People also live everyday lives:
- **Jobs:** each adult has a job, from hunter, farmer, fisher and herder to smith, potter, priest, healer, teacher, artist, factory worker, engineer and programmer. A job shapes what they do all day.
- **Needs:** people get tired and rest at home, and they get lonely and go chat with friends about the harvest, the war, the chief or news from across the sea.
- **Family:** people court and marry, couples raise children, elders tell stories to the young, and families mourn their dead at the town graveyard.
- **Festivals:** wealthy, pious towns hold festivals like the Lantern Festival or the Midsummer Feast, and everyone dances in the square.
- **Body language:** you can see people sit to rest, kneel to pray, dance, chop wood, hoe fields, fish with a rod and carry loads home. The Inspector shows each person's job, mood, partner, children and best friend.

### Each culture looks its own
Houses have doors, windows, roofs and decorations in their culture's style: Nordic longhouses with shields and carved gables, desert adobe with wooden beams and awnings, Eastern houses with red pillars and lanterns, Mediterranean villas with shutters and cypress trees, jungle houses with painted friezes, and Steppe yurts. Shutters, awnings and banners use each nation's own color. People wear their culture's traditional dress, and early navies sail Viking longships, Chinese junks and Nile feluccas.

### Nations and their AI
- **Council AI:** every year each nation sizes up its food, housing, neighbors and threats, then picks a plan: *grow, expand, fortify, arm for war, pursue knowledge* or *trade*. It learns from how well its last plan worked.
- **Rulers with personalities:** a ruler can be a Warlord, a Merchant, the Wise, or a Builder, and that tilts the nation's choices.
- **Six cultures:** Steppe, Nordic, Desert, Eastern, Mediterranean and Jungle. Each has its own names, homes, walls, wonders, dress and temperament.
- **Diplomacy:** relations rise and fall with borders, trade and faith. Nations form alliances, declare wars, surrender and pay tribute.
- **Faiths:** nations found religions that spread along trade routes. A shared faith brings nations closer, and a rival faith can start a holy war.

### War
![A general and his cavalry marching under a banner](docs/army.png)

- **Armies** muster, march in formation behind a general with a banner, and route around mountains and lakes.
- **Battles** are named after the land they're fought on, for example *"Battle of Batu Plain"*. An army that loses enough soldiers breaks and flees.
- **Sieges** wear down walls, then sack the town or conquer it outright. Defenders raise their own army to meet invaders.
- **Navies** of canoes, galleys, galleons, ironclads and destroyers fight at sea and bombard harbors.
- **Air power**: once a nation learns Flight it sends bombers and fighters into the sky. Planes dogfight, get shot down, and strike enemy towns. Propeller fighters give way to jet fighters that fire missiles.
- **Submarines** hunt under the waves, invisible until they surface or come close, and sink ships with torpedoes.
- **Artillery** rolls behind modern armies and shells the enemy, **helicopters** fly over them, and **anti-aircraft guns** in modern towns shoot down raiders.
- Weapons change with the era: spears and bronze, then cavalry, muskets, rifles and finally tanks.

### Explorers and the sea
Nations with Sailing send captains and settlers across the sea. When they reach an empty shore, they found an allied colony there. If their ship is sunk, the whole expedition is lost.

- **Islands** dot the open sea, and explorers are drawn to settle them.
- **Merchant ships** sail between harbors, from small trading cogs to container ships stacked with cargo. Each voyage trades goods, spreads ideas and builds friendship, and opens a sea route. Pirates sometimes seize a cargo.
- **Island communication** improves with the ages. Merchant ships carry the news at first. Then come undersea telegraph cables, radio, satellite links and fiber-optic internet cables. You can see cables on the seabed and messages flying through the sky. Linked nations grow friendlier and share ideas each year, and war cuts the link.
- **Harbor boats:** canoes and rafts, then rowboats and sailboats, steamers and tugs, and in modern times ferries, yachts and speedboats work around each harbor. Ferries cross between linked island harbors, and modern navies sail aircraft carriers with jets on deck.

  ![Ferries, a tug, a yacht and a sailboat in a modern harbor](docs/harbor.png)
- **Sea events change with the era.** Early fishers glimpse sea serpents and find beached whales. Later come pirate raids, treasure wrecks and icebergs in the shipping lanes. Industrial nations build offshore oil rigs, and in the 2000s you see cruise ships, oil spills, wind farms out at sea and the odd tsunami. Storms bring dark clouds, rain and lightning.
- **Sea life:** whales spout, dolphins leap, fish swirl in shoals and fishing boats work the coast near harbor towns.

### Towns that grow with the ages
![A Nordic town with a palisade, barracks, a market and grain silos](docs/town.png)

Stone Age families live in rock shelters and caves with a campfire and a hide rack out front. Towns then grow from huts to cities, and in the 2000s they pack into compact modern cities: a grid of streets with cars, glass towers that rise tallest at the center, rooftop gardens and antennas.

![A 2000s city of glass towers beside fields and forest](docs/city.png)

Out past the town edge, nations with railways build a **train station** where the line comes in, with platforms and a waiting train. With flight they build an **airport** with a runway, a terminal, a control tower, hangars and airliners that take off, circle and land.

![An airport with a runway, terminal, control tower and an airliner taking off](docs/airport.png)

**The space race:** modern nations build a launch pad with a service tower and send rockets up in clouds of smoke. They put satellites in orbit, then astronauts, then land on the Moon, build space stations, send probes and reach Mars. The Chronicle notes each first.

![A rocket lifting off from its launch pad](docs/launch.png)

Towns grow from huts to cities, and each culture builds its own style of home and wall. Over time they add **barracks, granaries, markets, forges, temples, castles, libraries, universities, observatories, arenas, windmills, hospitals, railway stations, statues, radio towers, airfields, data centers, power plants, wind turbines and stadiums**, plus three wonders per nation. Port towns build harbors and lighthouses. Roads link trading partners and get paved as the eras pass. **Caravans** travel the trade roads: pack mules and camels at first, then ox carts, covered wagons and finally trucks with their headlights on at night. They stop when the two nations go to war.

![A covered wagon on the trade road](docs/caravan.png)

![Eastern walls with tiled towers beside a pagoda town](docs/wall.png)

### Technology
There are **53 technologies** across seven eras. They run from Stone Tools, Fire and Agriculture through Bronze, Writing, Castles, Gunpowder, Printing, Steam, Railways, Electricity, Flight and Radio, up to Computers, Robotics, Satellites and Drones. Ideas spread between neighbors and trading partners. You can see what a nation knows on the map:

- **Railways:** trains run between trading towns.
- **Electricity:** power lines run between towns.
- **Flight:** airliners fly between friendly nations.
- **Robotics:** some workers are robots.
- **Satellites:** satellites circle high above the world.
- **Drones:** drones buzz over armies.

### A living world
- **Night lights:** with day and night on, towns light up after dark. Stone Age caves glow with campfires, early towns have a bonfire and torches by the doors and along the walls, the 1800s bring gas street lamps along roads, and modern cities have lit windows, streetlights, car headlights and red warning lights on tall towers. Lighthouses sweep the sea with beams and ships carry lanterns.

  ![A Middle Ages town at night lit by torches and a bonfire](docs/night.png)
- **Terrain:** low coastal plains, rolling hills, deep sea trenches and tall mountains of banded rock with ragged snow caps. The ground is patchy and textured rather than flat color, and the sea turns turquoise in the shallows, with foam where waves meet the shore.

  ![Rocky mountains with snow caps above forests and towns](docs/mountains.png)
- **Realistic finish:** on Medium and High the picture gets a filmic color grade and a soft glow on bright lights. High also adds ambient occlusion, which shades creases and the ground under buildings.
- **Biomes:** the north is frozen tundra and snowy pine forest, the middle is temperate woodland, and the south turns to savanna, jungle and desert, with swamps along wet coasts. Each biome has its own ground color, trees (palms and cacti, flat-topped acacias, dense jungle, snowy pines, willows), ground cover and wildlife (camels, zebras, elephants, reindeer). Deserts and tundra grow less food, so fewer people live there.
- **Natural models:** leafy trees have lumpy multi-lobed crowns that darken underneath, pines grow in layered tiers, palms have drooping fronds and coconuts, savanna acacias spread a wide layered canopy, and hill rocks are craggy boulders. Animals have rounded bodies, jointed legs, heads with ears and eyes, tails and antlers, and every ship and boat has a curved hull with a pointed bow.

  ![A Stone Age camp among palms, acacias and leafy trees](docs/trees.png)
- **Observatories:** once a nation learns astronomy, its astronomers build on the highest ground near town. Early on it is a raised stone sky platform with a gnomon and a turning armillary ring. Later come white domed observatories whose shutters open at night, with the dome slowly turning and the telescope peering out. By the 2000s there is a great radio telescope dish that sweeps the sky.

  ![Two domed observatories on a hill near town](docs/observatory.png)
- **Wartime forts:** a nation at war fortifies the side of town facing its nearest enemy, with its colors flying. It starts as a timber watchtower behind a palisade, then becomes a stone tower with battlements, a star fort with cannon, an earthwork redoubt with a field gun, a zigzag trench line with a concrete pillbox and barbed wire, and finally an air defense post with a turning radar and missile launcher.

  ![A stone watchtower flying its nation's flag outside a town at war](docs/fort.png)
- **Battlefields:** fights out in the countryside leave their mark. The ground is trampled and scorched, gunpowder battles leave craters, and wreckage of the era lies around: broken spears and shields, toppled cannons and overturned carts, then burnt-out tanks and wire. The scars fade over a few years, but a great battle leaves a memorial stone with rows of graves.

  ![Scorched ground and a burnt-out tank after a battle](docs/battlefield.png)
- **Shipyards:** harbor towns build a shipyard on the shore beside the harbor. A new ship takes shape on the slipway, first the keel, then the frames, then the planks or steel plates, and then it slides down into the sea before the next one is started. Early yards have timber scaffolding and a swinging wooden crane, and modern ones have a yellow gantry crane rolling along rails over the slip.

  ![A wooden ship being framed on a slipway with scaffolding and a crane](docs/shipyard.png)
- **Power stations:** industrial towns build a power station out of town. In the 1800s it is a brick hall with three tall chimneys and a coal heap, by the 1900s a cooling tower breathes clouds of steam beside a single stack, and in the 2000s some nations run nuclear plants with a containment dome and twin cooling towers. Every plant has a switchyard and pylons.

  ![A power station with a chimney and a cooling tower giving off steam](docs/power.png)
- **Quarries and mines:** once a town leaves the Stone Age it digs into the nearest hill or mountain outside town. Early towns cut stone in stepped quarries with a swinging wooden crane, later ones drive timbered mine shafts with ore carts on rails and spoil heaps, industrial towns raise winding towers with turning wheels and coal heaps, and modern mines have steel headframes and dump trucks.

  ![A mine with a winding tower, ore cart and coal heap outside a snowy town](docs/mine.png)
- **Town buildings:** towns grow new buildings as they age and get richer: totems and standing stones, wells, pottery kilns, aqueducts, bathhouses, ziggurats, watermills, taverns, monasteries, town halls, schools, banks, factories, water towers, fire stations, museums, parks, shopping malls, solar farms and TV towers.

  ![An 1800s town with factories, a museum and a school](docs/buildings.png)
- **Weather:** some clouds drift in low and grey and drop rain showers as they pass, with the odd lightning bolt. Over snowy lands, high mountains and in winter they snow instead. Deserts stay dry.

  ![A grey cloud snowing over a winter town](docs/snow.png)
- Seasons change every year: green summers, autumn colors and snowy winters.
- Day turns to night: the sun sets in orange, the sky goes dark and lanterns and windows light up in every town. You can turn this off with **Day and night** in the panel.
- Wild herds of deer and boar graze the wilderness and disappear as towns claim the land.
- Sheep, goats, cattle and llamas graze around farming towns, depending on the culture.
- Birds circle overhead, chimneys smoke (factory smoke is darker), and clouds drift past with their shadows.

### History
Everything that happens goes into the **Chronicle**: wars, battles, sieges, golden ages, rebellions, new faiths, discoveries, wonders and the fall of nations. Charts track the world's population and each nation's rise and fall.

## Controls

| Action | How |
| --- | --- |
| Orbit, pan or zoom | Drag, right-drag, or scroll |
| Inspect a person or town | Click it on the map |
| Follow someone | Select them, then **Follow with camera** |
| Follow an army | Click it in the **Armies** panel or its map label |
| Speed | Pause, Slow, Normal, Fast or Max |
| Reshape the world | Meteor, Earthquake, Plague, Wildfire, Drought, Harvest, Newcomers, Volcano, Flood or Grow forest, then click the map |
| Meddle with a nation | Golden age, Gift knowledge, Prophet, Broker peace, Sow war, Raise army or Rebellion, then click near a town |
| Graphics | **Auto**, **Low** (phones and older PCs), **Medium** or **High**. Auto picks a level for your device and steps down if the game runs slowly. Your choice is remembered. |
| Save or load | **Save world** and **Load saved** store your world in this browser |

## How it's built

- **One file:** `index.html` holds both the simulation and the 3D view. [three.js](https://threejs.org) r147 loads from a CDN.
- **Simulation:** the code inside `<script id="sim-core">` is pure logic with no DOM, so it runs headless in Node. Everything that happens goes through `sim.emit()`, which feeds the Chronicle.
- **Rendering:** models are built from instanced primitive parts, so thousands of people, buildings and animals draw in a handful of draw calls.

## Tests

The simulation runs headless in Node:

```bash
node tests/run.js [era] [seed] [years]   # run a world and print stats and history
node tests/ai.js                         # council AI strategies
node tests/nudge.js                      # world tools (meteor, plague, ...)
node tests/tools.js                      # new tools (volcano, golden age, peace, ...)
node tests/war2.js                       # jets, submarines and anti-aircraft
node tests/sea.js                        # islands, merchant ships and sea links
node tests/life.js                       # jobs, marriage, festivals and everyday life
node tests/save.js                       # save and load round trip
node tests/army.js                       # armies, battles and sieges in four eras
```
